# Asset notes

The 48 bird images are individual AI-generated natural-history illustrations with transparent backgrounds. The maple, mahogany, and cherry textures are AI-generated wood studies. They are bundled as compressed WebP files and load from this site without external services. They are visual design references, not authoritative taxonomic plates or guaranteed samples of particular timber.

The original twelve species and arrangement follow the accompanying Figma design. Twenty additional birds include the Mourning dove requested earlier. Kōkako represents the original New Zealand blue-bird choice.

Sixteen more birds were generated with the built-in imagegen tool on October 17, 2026, in the same natural-history illustration style. Their transparent outputs are compressed to 600 × 600 WebP assets. The full prompt template, individual subject prompts, asset paths, and species-name reference links are recorded in `assets/bird-generation-2026-10.json`. These illustrations were generated individually, not retrieved from a dataset or copied from the reference sites.

Region labels identify a primary browsing association, not a species' complete geographic range. Bird groups are informal browsing categories rather than a taxonomic classification. Both filters derive their available choices from the catalogue, and user uploads appear under Custom.

Local typography: Cormorant Garamond and Inter, downloaded from Google Fonts. Their SIL Open Font License texts are included in `assets/fonts/OFL-Cormorant-Garamond.txt` and `assets/fonts/OFL-Inter.txt`. Keep those license files with redistributed font assets.

Display copies are generated by `scripts/optimize-assets.py` using Pillow, fonttools, and brotli. Bird previews are 256 px, with 384 px variants for enlarged placements; wood previews are 384 px. The original WebP artwork and textures are unchanged and remain the source for downloads and clock PNG exports. WOFF2 fonts use separate Latin and extended-Latin subsets, so extended characters load only when visible. Run the asset script after changing artwork or fonts, then `npm run build`.

User-uploaded artwork remains in browser storage and exported JSON. Use images you have permission to share.

The download panel can export a bird in PNG, JPEG, WebP, or SVG at its stored resolution. SVG files embed a PNG copy of the raster artwork and do not contain traced vector paths. JPEG uses a white background; PNG, WebP, and SVG retain any existing transparency. WebP originals are preserved byte-for-byte. ZIP collections include species names, file dimensions, and the hours assigned in the current design. Uploaded artwork is exported from its saved copy, resized to at most 512 px when uploaded.

## Researched bird profiles

`assets/bird-profiles.json` contains original summaries researched on October 17, 2026. Each profile links its sources and identifies which fields they support. References include the Cornell Lab of Ornithology, New Zealand Birds Online, RSPB, BirdLife, World Parrot Trust, International Crane Foundation, and zoological institutions. Source articles, photographs, and licensed range polygons are not copied into the project. Bird audio is separately sourced and licensed as described below.

Measurements are adult reference values; ranges may reflect sex or geographic variation, and single values are approximate. Cranes have height measurements rather than bill-to-tail length. The peafowl length includes the male's train. Conservation categories retain their source system and geographic scope; the review date is not the original assessment date. The North American barn owl is identified as _Tyto furcata_ following the American barn owl split; its saved-design identifier remains `barn-owl`. Kōkako refers to the North Island species, _Callaeas wilsoni_.

## Occurrence maps

Local PNG layers in `assets/maps` are snapshots rendered by the [GBIF Maps API](https://techdocs.gbif.org/en/openapi/v2/maps), retrieved on October 17, 2026. `manifest.json` records exact species keys, source pages, map URLs, and regional tile coordinates. Each profile links back to its GBIF species page. Credits belong to GBIF and the contributing data publishers; their underlying records retain the licenses shown by GBIF. These rendered maps are an educational overview, not a licensed range dataset or a substitute for a citable occurrence-data download.

`world.svg` is a Web Mercator rendering of the [Natural Earth 1:110m land polygons](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/blob/master/geojson/ne_110m_land.geojson). Natural Earth data is [public domain](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/). The SVG is simplified geographic context, with no claims about political boundaries.

GBIF records span all years, have uneven reporting coverage, and can include captive, introduced, historical, or misidentified birds. Map density is not population abundance. Regional framing is editorial and does not define a native range; the text explains distribution separately. No maps are generated from the catalogue's broad region filter.

To refresh occurrence maps, run `python3 scripts/update-bird-maps.py` with network access. The script uses Python's standard library, requires exact species matches, finishes downloading before replacing assets, and updates the manifest, map date, taxonomy, and map references. Empty GBIF tiles are omitted. Review the resulting taxonomy and geographic changes, run the data/browser tests, and inspect regional and world views before publishing. This does not re-research prose, measurements, conservation, or their review dates. Update those manually from the linked sources when needed.

## Bird recordings

46 short recordings were selected on October 17, 2026. These are real recorded vocalizations, not generated audio. Their sources are Wikimedia Commons, Xeno-canto, and iNaturalist, including recordings contributed by the British Library, New Zealand Department of Conservation, US National Park Service, and US Fish and Wildlife Service. Each recording retains its own public-domain status, CC BY, or CC BY-SA license; the audio is not covered by a blanket project license.

The complete, user-facing attribution is in [assets/calls/credits.html](assets/calls/credits.html). Keep that page and `assets/bird-audio.json` with redistributed clips. They list the recordist, source title and URL, Creative Commons license/version, excerpt start, and modifications. Xeno-canto record IDs and original archive links are preserved where available. Each MP3 also embeds its credit, license URL, and source URL. Adapted clips retain their source license, including ShareAlike where applicable.

`assets/bird-audio-sources.json` is the manually curated input. Search results were checked for species identity and recording type; pronunciations, music compositions, other foreground species, unclear attribution, restricted files, and NC/ND licenses were excluded. The three iNaturalist clips use the **sound's** CC BY license, not the observation's license. Red-crowned crane and Rainbow lorikeet have research-grade community identifications. Golden pheasant has multiple matching identifications and is explicitly marked captive. iNaturalist's [official license mapping](https://github.com/inaturalist/inaturalist/blob/main/app/models/shared/license_module.rb) specifies CC 4.0.

Snowy owl and secretarybird remain unavailable: this curation did not establish suitable, accessible, freely reusable recordings for them. This does not claim that no such recording exists. No recording is assigned to uploaded artwork. Natural background sounds may remain in the clips; metadata and labels describe the source record, not automated species recognition.

The preparation script fetches only explicitly selected URLs, at concurrency two, and checks SHA-256 hashes of cached originals. It makes up-to-12-second, mono 44.1 kHz / 64 kbit/s MP3 excerpts, normalizes loudness to a target of −20 LUFS with a −2 dB true-peak limit, and adds brief fades. Source timing, output duration, bytes, and output hashes are recorded. Original downloads stay in the system temporary directory (`birdclock-audio-originals`) unless `--cache-dir` is supplied; they are not committed. To replace a source, review its identity/license, update its URL and source hash, remove its old cached original, and regenerate. Do not substitute an unverified recording to fill a coverage gap.

Reference policies: [Wikimedia reuse](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:First_steps/Reuse), [Xeno-canto terms](https://xeno-canto.org/about/terms), and [iNaturalist sound-license filters](https://help.inaturalist.org/en/support/solutions/articles/151000170738-how-to-use-inaturalist-search-urls). Runtime playback is entirely local to this site; no external API, account, or API key is required.
